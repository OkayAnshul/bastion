# 1.4 PR-AUC vs ROC-AUC when fraud is rare

**Phase:** 1 · **Date:** 2026-09-14 · **Time spent:** written during the build

> **About the numbers.** Measured values come from the synthetic test window (`bastion train`). The
> "flagging 758 transactions" calculation is illustrative arithmetic. IEEE-CIS results are
> **unmeasured**.

## What problem does this solve?

Before any decision threshold exists, we need to compare scorers (rules, logistic regression,
LightGBM) by how well they *rank* fraud above legitimate traffic. Two summary metrics are standard,
ROC-AUC and PR-AUC. With fraud under 1% of transactions they tell very different stories, and only one
of them tracks what an analyst experiences: of the cases I'm shown, how many are real fraud?

## How it works

- **ROC** plots recall (true-positive rate) against the false-positive rate, **FP / all legitimate
  transactions**. That denominator is enormous, so thousands of false alarms barely move it.
- **Precision-recall** plots precision, **TP / all flagged**, against recall. False alarms hit precision
  directly.

*Illustrative arithmetic on the synthetic test window* (47,361 transactions, 0.76% fraud, so about 360
frauds and about 47,000 legitimate):

A scorer that catches 80% of fraud (288 cases) at a 1% false-positive rate also flags about 470
legitimate customers. On ROC that point (TPR 0.80, FPR 0.01) looks superb. Its precision is
288 / (288 + 470) ≈ **38%**: nearly two of every three cases an analyst opens are innocent.

**Measured on the same window:**

| Scorer | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC [95% CI] | No-skill PR-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rules baseline (rules fired) | 0.7838 | 0.1970 [0.163, 0.238] | 0.0076 |
| Logistic regression | 0.9986 | 0.9485 [0.927, 0.964] | 0.0076 |
| LightGBM + Platt | 1.0000 | 1.0000 [1.000, 1.000] | 0.0076 |

The rules' ROC-AUC of 0.78 sounds respectable, while a PR-AUC of 0.20 says most of what they rank highly
isn't fraud. Logistic regression's ROC-AUC of 0.9986 looks nearly perfect; its precision-recall curve
(`figures/model_precision_recall.png`) holds near 1.0 up to about 85% recall and then falls away
steeply.

**Details that matter:**

- A PR-AUC's **no-skill value is the positive rate** (0.0076 here); ROC-AUC's is always 0.5. A
  PR-AUC is meaningless without its base rate next to it, and every Bastion report prints both.
- Bastion's PR-AUC is **average precision**: precision at each threshold, weighted by the recall gained
  there. It is a step sum with no interpolation. Linear interpolation between precision-recall points
  is wrong, because precision does not change linearly between thresholds (Davis and Goadrich, 2006).
  That is why the figure is drawn as steps.
- **Bootstrap intervals** (200 resamples of the test rows) keep us from reading meaning into
  differences smaller than the sampling noise.

## Why we chose it here

- **ADR-005** makes money the headline: fraud value caught, false-decline rate, loss at a review
  budget. PR-AUC is the threshold-free ranking metric for comparing scorers *before* those decisions
  exist, and it is the one that punishes false alarms.
- Reports show PR-AUC and ROC-AUC side by side, precisely so the contrast is visible.

## What we rejected and why

- **Accuracy.** Approving everything scores 99.24% on this window.
- **ROC-AUC as the headline.** It is dominated by the huge legitimate class and flatters scorers
  that would bury analysts in false alarms.
- **F1.** It fixes one threshold, weights precision and recall equally, and ignores amounts and
  review capacity.

## Where it breaks

- **PR-AUC depends on the base rate.** A window with more fraud scores higher with no change in the
  model. Compare PR-AUC only on the same window, or always next to the no-skill value.
- **It averages over thresholds nobody uses.** With capacity for N reviews a day, only the top of the
  ranking matters. That calls for precision at k, or value caught at a review budget (Phase 4).
- **It counts, it doesn't weigh.** Missing a 3 USD probe costs PR-AUC as much as missing a 2,000 USD
  cash-out.
- **At the ceiling it is uninformative.** On synthetic data several scorers reach 1.0000 and the
  metric cannot separate them.

## The interview answer

"With fraud under one percent, ROC-AUC mostly measures how well you order the legitimate majority.
Thousands of false alarms barely move the false-positive rate. Precision-recall exposes them directly.
On my synthetic data the rules scored a ROC-AUC of 0.78 but a PR-AUC of 0.20. So I compare scorers on
PR-AUC with bootstrap intervals and the base rate printed beside it, and I make the actual business
decision on money at a fixed review budget."
