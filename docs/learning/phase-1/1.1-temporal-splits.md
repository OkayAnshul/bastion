# 1.1 Temporal splits and label maturity

**Phase:** 1 · **Date:** 2026-09-14 · **Time spent:** written during the build

> **About the numbers.** Worked examples come from the synthetic table (`bastion data synthetic
> --days 183 --cards 3000 --seed 7`, then `bastion train`). Every IEEE-CIS figure is **unmeasured**
> until the dataset is downloaded.

## What problem does this solve?

A fraud model is always used on transactions that happen *after* it was trained. An honest
evaluation has to reproduce that: fit on the past, measure on the future, and never let the model see
information that did not exist when it was trained. That includes labels that had not arrived yet.
A random split breaks both rules. It puts next week's transactions, and members of the same fraud
ring, into the training set, and it treats every label as known the moment the transaction happened.

## How it works

**Windows, not rows.** `configs/splits.yaml` cuts the timeline into contiguous calendar-day windows.
`bastion.data.splits.assign_splits` labels each row by event time only, and row order is never
touched.

| Window | Days | Rows (synthetic) | Fraud rate | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `train` | 0–98 | 161,328 | 0.72% | model, feature vocabularies, rule thresholds |
| `maturity_gap` | 98–112 | 23,170 | 0.59% | nothing: its labels were not final at the cutoff |
| `early_stopping` | 112–133 | 34,847 | 0.74% | choosing the number of boosting rounds |
| `calibration` | 133–154 | 34,993 | 0.75% | fitting and choosing the calibrator; policy thresholds (Phase 4) |
| `test` | 154–183 | 47,361 | 0.76% | reported once |

**Label maturity.** A label exists only when it has *arrived*. `bastion.data.labels` simulates the
arrival time:

- A fraud label arrives after a lognormal chargeback delay: median 5 days, shape 0.6, capped at 14
  days. The cap binds for P(z > ln(14/5)/0.6 ≈ 1.72) ≈ 4.3% of frauds; a unit test confirms the
  capped share falls between 2% and 7% over 6,000 draws.
- A legitimate label "arrives" when 14 days pass with no chargeback.
- The delay is a hash of `txn_id` and a seed, so batch training and the stream replay agree on every
  arrival time.

```
day:   ... 96     97     98 ......................... 112     113 ...
            |------ train ------|---- maturity gap ----|-- early_stopping --
fraud on day 97, 13-day delay:   label arrives day 110 ─┘  (inside the gap: fine)
legitimate on day 97:            label final on day 111 ─┘  (inside the gap: fine)
fraud on day 105 (gap row):      label arrives day 118, after early_stopping has started:
                                 that is exactly why gap rows are not trained on
```

**The guard.** `check_label_maturity` fails training if any training label arrives after the first
`early_stopping` transaction. A unit test shrinks the gap to 7 days and asserts that training
refuses to run.

## Why we chose it here

- **ADR-007** makes temporal splits non-negotiable. ARCHITECTURE.md describes a 12-week dataset;
  IEEE-CIS covers about six months, so the same train / validate / test shape is applied
  proportionally.
- In IEEE-CIS, fraud labels propagate *forward* within an account (`docs/data_dictionary.md`). A
  random split would put an account's later, labelled transactions into training and its earlier
  ones into test.
- The gap costs data: 23,170 synthetic rows are never trained on. That is the price of not training
  on labels from the future.

## What we rejected and why

- **`train_test_split(shuffle=True)`.** It lets the model see the future. A hygiene test fails the
  build if it appears anywhere except the leakage experiment, which reproduces it on purpose.
- **k-fold cross-validation.** Every fold mixes time periods. Rolling-origin (time-series)
  cross-validation would be honest, but it multiplies training cost, and we don't tune hyper-
  parameters here. It remains a reasonable upgrade.
- **No maturity gap.** Recent frauds whose chargebacks had not landed would be trained on as
  legitimate.
- **Choosing anything on `test`.** Early stopping, the calibrator, and the rule and policy thresholds
  all use earlier windows. The test window is read once, at the end.

## Where it breaks

- **The delay is simulated.** Real chargebacks can take months; the IEEE-CIS provider mentions
  120 days. A realistic gap would consume a large share of a six-month dataset.
- **One test window is one sample of time.** Bootstrap intervals cover sampling noise inside the
  window, not variation between periods. A seasonal shift can make the next window look nothing
  like this one.
- **The separation is by time, not by entity.** The same cards appear in train and test. That is
  realistic for production, but card-level labels in IEEE-CIS can still inform later rows through
  label propagation.
- **Synthetic ceilings hide the effect.** On synthetic data the shuffled split scored 0.9978 against
  the temporal split's 1.0000. When both sit at the ceiling, the difference is noise, not evidence.

## The interview answer

"I split by time, never randomly: train on the first 98 days, skip a 14-day maturity gap, early-stop
on the next three weeks, calibrate on the three after that, and report once on the last month. The
gap exists because fraud labels arrive weeks late. At the training cutoff, recent transactions look
legitimate only because their chargebacks haven't landed. Training fails outright if any training
label would have arrived after evaluation starts, so that mistake can't creep back in."
